    Course Description¶

    Dakhotiyapi — Beginning Dakota Language (Year 1)¶

    Level: High School Introductory
    Duration: 160 instructional days (one academic year)
    Prerequisites: None
    Cross-listed with: AIS 112 — American Indian Studies

    Course Overview¶

    This course introduces students to the Dakota language alongside the history, culture, and sovereignty of the Očhéthi Šakówiŋ (Seven Council Fires). Each chapter pairs language learning with AIS 112 content, reinforcing that Dakota language and Dakota history are inseparable.

    Semester Structure¶

    Semester 1 (Chapters 1–4)¶

    Chapter Dakota Title Topics AIS 112 Context
    1 Háu / Haŋ Greetings, identity, tiyóšpaye Očhéthi Šakówiŋ, pre-contact society
    2 Otóiyohi Numbers, colors, descriptors Trade networks, wampum, counting systems
    3 Wóuŋsipi Daily life, action verbs Seasonal calendar, bison economy
    4 Čhaŋté Body, health, relationships Medicine traditions, kinship systems

    Mid-Year Exam — Chapters 1–4 | ACTFL Novice Low–Mid

    Semester 2 (Chapters 5–8)¶

    Chapter Dakota Title Topics AIS 112 Context
    5 Wóčhekiye Prayer, values, the sacred Mitákuye Oyásʼiŋ, spiritual traditions
    6 Makóčhe Land, seasons, directions Seasonal lifeways, celestial knowledge
    7 Wóoyake Story, ceremony, oral tradition Oral tradition, ceremony, suppression
    8 Oyáte Nation, treaties, sovereignty 1862 War, Fort Snelling, treaty rights

    End-of-Year Exam — Chapters 5–8 | ACTFL Novice Mid–High

    ACTFL Proficiency Targets¶

    Level Description Target
    Novice Low Isolated words and memorized phrases Chapters 1–2
    Novice Mid Lists of words, some phrase combining Chapters 3–4 / Mid-Year
    Novice High Short phrases, some spontaneous production Chapters 5–7
    Intermediate Low Simple sentences, creates with language Chapter 8 / End of Year

    The Očhéthi Šakówiŋ — Seven Council Fires¶

    The Dakota course situates the language within the broader Očhéthi Šakówiŋ (Seven Council Fires), the political and cultural alliance of seven related peoples:

    Council Fire Common Name Dialect
    Bdewákhaŋthuŋwaŋ Mdewakanton Dakota
    Waȟpékhute Wahpekute Dakota
    Sisíthuŋwaŋ Sisseton Dakota
    Waȟpéthuŋwaŋ Wahpeton Dakota
    Iháŋkthuŋwaŋ Yankton Nakota
    Iháŋkthuŋwaŋna Yanktonai Nakota
    Thítȟuŋwaŋ Teton / Lakota Lakota

    This course teaches Dakota (the eastern dialect), the language of the Minnesota communities.

    Learning Outcomes¶

    After completing this course, students will be able to:

    Remember¶

    • Recall the gender-specific greeting forms (Háu / Haŋ) and their appropriate use
    • List the numbers 1–1,000 in Dakota
    • Name the Seven Council Fires (Očhéthi Šakówiŋ) and their dialect groupings
    • Identify the Standard Dakota Orthography characters and accent marks
    • Recall the four cardinal directions in Dakota
    • Name the seven Dakota values (wačháŋtognaka, ohítika, ksapá, and others)
    • Recognize the Dakota words for seasons, body parts, family relationships, and daily activities
    • List the key events of the U.S.-Dakota War of 1862 in sequence

    Understand¶

    • Explain the Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) word order of Dakota and how it differs from English
    • Describe how stative verbs function as color and quality descriptors in Dakota
    • Explain the cultural significance of the tiyóšpaye (extended family band) as the basic social unit
    • Summarize the events of the U.S.-Dakota War of 1862, the Fort Snelling imprisonment, and the Mankato executions
    • Explain the concept of Mitákuye Oyásʼiŋ (All my relations) and its worldview implications
    • Describe how the passive name construction (emáčiyapi) reflects Dakota values
    • Explain how treaty language affected Dakota land, sovereignty, and community continuity
    • Summarize the role of oral tradition (wóoyake) in Dakota cultural transmission

    Apply¶

    • Introduce themselves in Dakota using name, origin, and formal closing
    • Form future tense (-kte) and negation (-šni) on Dakota verbs correctly
    • Use Dakota vocabulary to describe seasons, land, and directions in context
    • Apply the SOV sentence structure to construct original Dakota sentences
    • Use stative verbs to describe colors and qualities in Dakota
    • Count and perform basic arithmetic communication in Dakota (1–1,000)
    • Apply gender-appropriate speech forms in greeting contexts

    Analyze¶

    • Compare Dakota SOV grammar to English SVO word order and explain the cognitive shift required
    • Distinguish between stative verbs and action verbs in Dakota grammar
    • Examine how the 1851 and 1858 treaties reduced Dakota land and examine the legal and cultural consequences
    • Analyze how Dakota vocabulary encodes a relational and land-based worldview
    • Compare Dakota and English name construction and explain what each reveals about cultural values
    • Analyze the role of the boarding school era in disrupting Dakota language transmission

    Evaluate¶

    • Assess the significance of language revitalization efforts for Dakota communities today
    • Evaluate the impact of U.S. federal Indian policy (boarding schools, Code of Indian Offenses) on Dakota language and culture
    • Judge the role of oral tradition and ceremony in preserving cultural knowledge that was legally suppressed
    • Evaluate the ethical responsibilities of non-Dakota learners engaging with the language

    Create¶

    • Compose a formal Dakota self-introduction (name, origin, closing) suitable for a community context
    • Write a short paragraph about a season or place using Dakota vocabulary and SOV structure
    • Design a short dialogue between two speakers using greetings, identity phrases, and at least one future tense sentence
    • Produce a cultural reflection connecting a language concept to a historical event or contemporary issue
    • Draft a written response to the question: "Why does Dakota language revitalization matter?"

    Topics Excluded¶

    This course does NOT cover:

    • Lakota or Nakota dialects (cultural and political context provided, but language instruction focuses on Dakota only)
    • Advanced Dakota grammar beyond Novice High / early Intermediate Low ACTFL level
    • Writing systems other than the Standard Dakota Orthography
    • Ceremonially restricted cultural content
    • Speaking fluency beyond basic communicative phrases at the Intermediate Low threshold
    • Detailed comparative linguistics of the Siouan language family

    Component Weight
    Chapter Quizzes (8) 30%
    Mid-Year Exam 20%
    End-of-Year Exam 20%
    Participation / Oral Practice 20%
    Cultural Reflection Journals 10%
