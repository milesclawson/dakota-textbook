## Part B — Fill in the Blank (3 questions, 5 points each)

??? question "11. The Dakota word for prayer — the ongoing practice of maintaining relationship with all that is sacred — is __________."
    ??? answer "Answer"
        **wóčhekiye**

        *wóčhekiye* is both a noun (prayer, an act of prayer) and describes the practice of relational prayerfulness. It is broader than the English "prayer," encompassing ongoing relationship with the sacred rather than only formal petition.

??? question "12. Complete the sentence: *Wičhášta kiŋ ksapá __________.*  (The man is wise.)"
    ??? answer "Answer"
        The sentence *Wičhášta kiŋ ksapá* is already complete as a predicate construction.

        However, if the exercise asks for the existential verb to make the predicate explicit: **waúŋ** → *Wičhášta kiŋ ksapá waúŋ.* — The man exists in a state of wisdom.

        (Note: in Dakota, stative verbs like *ksapá* can stand alone as predicates without a linking verb; *waúŋ* makes the construction more explicit.)

??? question "13. The four cardinal Dakota virtues are: __________, __________, __________, and __________."
    ??? answer "Answer"
        1. **wačháŋtognaka** (generosity)
        2. **ohítika** (bravery)
        3. **ksapá** (wisdom)
        4. **čhaŋtéčhila** (love / cherishing)

        These four virtues were publicly demonstrated through community action — give-aways, acts of protection, council wisdom, and the raising of children with care.
